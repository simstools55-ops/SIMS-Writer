# Revenue First Validation Principle v3.1.0

## Purpose

SIMS Writer is an SEO improvement system for individual blog operators whose practical objective is sustainable click and revenue growth. Validation must therefore prevent changes that are likely to reduce search performance, confuse search intent, damage a winning query, or publish materially false or harmful information. It must not pursue editorial perfection that has negligible SEO or revenue impact.

## Priority order

1. Reader safety and material factual accuracy.
2. Primary search intent and title/body promise alignment.
3. Winner-query and existing traffic asset preservation.
4. Click opportunity, CTR presentation and query-cluster alignment.
5. Internal-link routing, cannibalization control and page-role separation.
6. Contract, Before/After and JSON integrity.
7. Optional prose polish.

Items 1–6 may affect publication. Item 7 does not block publication by itself.

## Revenue-critical finding

A finding is revenue-critical only when the proposed change could materially:

- lower clicks or CTR by weakening a proven query/title asset;
- shift the page toward a different search intent;
- create a title or snippet promise the article does not fulfil;
- cause cannibalization, broken routing or a non-functional link;
- publish a central false, stale or unsafe claim;
- corrupt the final copy or the SBM JSON registration state.

## Non-blocking quality finding

The following remain internal recommendations unless they create one of the risks above:

- alternative wording that is merely more cautious or elegant;
- punctuation, sentence length, tone or stylistic preference;
- optional evidence detail that does not change the claim's truth or reader decision;
- optional FAQ or internal-link opportunities;
- minor prose improvements with no material search impact.

## User-decision boundary

User decision is reserved for genuine owner choices such as:

- protecting a proven query versus testing a materially different title;
- merging, splitting, redirecting or canonically consolidating pages;
- publishing first-party experience, commercial policy or brand claims;
- selecting a monetization or affiliate-routing strategy;
- confirming a fact only the site owner can verify.

The user must not be used as the system's copy editor or repair engine.
